A watershed is the area of land that drains to a single point. That definition is the easy part. The hard part is that the point is your choice, and so is the resolution at which you trace the boundary, and so is the data you start from.

## Three watersheds, same valley

Trace the watershed of a small mountain creek using a 30-metre DEM. Do it again with a 10-metre DEM. Do it again with the 1-metre LiDAR product the province released last year. You will get three different polygons, sometimes wildly so. The 30-metre boundary will average over small features. The LiDAR boundary will follow drainage channels you can stand inside.

None of the three is wrong. They are all answering different questions.

## The downstream point matters

Where you place the outlet matters more than which DEM you use. Move the outlet five hundred metres downstream and the boundary you compute can shift by hectares. This is not a bug; it is the geometry of how water moves. But it does mean that "the watershed of the Bow" is not a single object — it is a family of objects parametrised by where you decided to stop.
